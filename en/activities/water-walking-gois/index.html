Water Walking in Góis

River Ceira · Góis

Discover the River Ceira from its riverbed, among water, rock and riverside vegetation. Water Walking combines walking and swimming, small rapids and natural obstacles. Jumps are optional, with alternative ways to progress.

Why choose this experience

Explore the River Ceira from within, among water, rock and woodland, on a route that combines walking and swimming.

Activity photographs

When the river becomes the route

Water Walking follows the bed of the River Ceira in Góis. Water, rocks and vegetation along the banks shape the experience: walking sections, swimming crossings and small rapids appear along the route. The river sets the pace. Progress changes from one stretch to the next; the team accompanies the obstacles and jumps have alternatives, so everyone does not have to take the same option.

Your adventure, without compulsory jumps

Jumps are optional, with alternative ways to progress. The programme is adapted to river conditions and the group following the technical team's assessment.

Along the route

  • Walking and swimming
  • Small rapids and natural obstacles
  • Alternative ways past the jumps

Prepare your enquiry

Include your preferred date and number of participants. Ask for the total price including VAT and the applicable terms; an enquiry does not confirm a booking.

Questions to ask the team

  • What do the four hours include, and how much time is spent on the route?
  • What physical preparation is needed for walking, swimming and moving past the river’s obstacles?
  • Where and when is the meeting? If I arrive without a car, what options are available for that outing?
  • Which language can the activity be guided in?
Read about preparation and safety

Before you choose

Do I have to jump?

No. Jumps are optional and there are alternative ways to progress along the route.

How long does it take?

The approximate duration is four hours. The programme is adapted to river conditions and the group.

Is the whole route on foot?

No. Water walking combines walking along the riverbed with swimming.

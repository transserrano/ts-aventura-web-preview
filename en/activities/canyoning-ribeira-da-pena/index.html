Canyoning · Pena

Góis

A descent through a deep, enclosed valley in Góis, with waterfalls, pools and rocky outcrops. Progression combines water and ropes; jumps and slides are optional.

    Where
    Ribeira da Pena · Góis
    Experience
    Canyoning
    Duration
    Around 4–5 hours.
    Age and participation
    Ages 12 and over.
    Minimum participants
    Minimum of 4 paying participants.
    Meeting point
    9:30 am, in Ribeira Cimeira, Góis.
    Recommended season
    Spring, summer and autumn; winter trips depend on conditions.
    Logistics
    A walk of around 30 minutes out after the canyoning route.

    What you will experience

    Rock, dense vegetation and the Ribeira da Pena stream shape a journey through and alongside the water. Changes in height call for different techniques along the route, rather than a simple walk beside the stream.

    Along the route

    Entry may include an optional abseil of about 35 metres, chosen by the team according to the group and conditions. Aquatic passages, scrambling and abseils follow, with optional jumps or slides. The descent ends with an exit walk to Ribeira Cimeira.

    Included equipment & guiding

    • Neoprene suit and socks, helmet, harness and technical canyoning equipment. Waterproof packs and containers allow small belongings to be carried
    • a buoyancy aid is used where needed.

    What to bring

    Bring water, a snack or light lunch, swimwear, dry clothes, towel, flip-flops and substantial-soled trainers for moving through the water. Prepare a retaining strap for glasses.

    Requirements & preparation

    Requires mobility and physical fitness suitable for progressing over uneven terrain, through water and during technical manoeuvres. Injuries, mobility limitations, pregnancy or relevant health conditions must be disclosed in advance. Participation is subject to assessment by the technical team.

    Meeting & logistics

    Ribeira Cimeira in Góis. The programme arranges transfers between start and finish and an exit walk from the stream.

    Questions before you decide

    Are jumps and natural slides always part of canyoning?

    No. They are described as optional. The technical team explains which manoeuvres and alternatives apply to the outing.
